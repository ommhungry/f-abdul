## 2. The advantages matrix

"Lands in" points to the section of `combined-proposal.md` that uses the idea.

### 2.1 omm (obligations assistant, governance, architecture)

| ID | Idea | Verdict | Why | Lands in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| O1 | **Obligation lifecycle with evidence levels** (captured → confirmed → action pending → user-reported → evidence attached → externally verified → uncertain) | **Keep, and widen** | It is the clearest, most testable difference from a reminder-only product (validation-omm-brief §5 item 1). It generalises to requests: a business answering on our reply page is a new, stronger evidence level than "the user says so". One lifecycle now covers both obligations and requests | §4.2, §8.4, §9 |
| O2 | **"AI interprets; code decides"** (the model cannot grant permission, send, or mark success) | **Keep as the top design rule** | It is the safety backbone for any assistant that acts. It also makes the ETA s.15 para 2(2) risk manageable: nothing leaves the system without a user tap that code checks | §4.3, §7 |
| O3 | **Exact-offer approvals** (an approval binds the exact content; any material change needs a new approval) | **Adapt** | In the prototype there is no price or deposit. The approval binds the exact request-card text, business, and time options (a content hash). Editing after approval voids it | §4.3, §7.2 |
| O4 | **Family sharing, one item at a time** (item-level grant, separate read / complete permissions, revocation) | **Adapt → Should** | It is a recorded team direction (omm brief header) and buildable in a minimal form. But it does not fit the Must hours together with the request card (§6.3). Minimal version: share one item with one invited adult, read status and mark done, revoke | §6.1 (S1) |
| O5 | **Core data model** (§8.2: separate extracted claim with confidence from the confirmed obligation; approval; audit event; notification) | **Keep, extend** | Separating the extracted claim from the confirmed record makes extraction accuracy measurable (validation-omm-brief §5 item 9). | O6 | **Webhook reliability** (signature over raw body, constant-time compare, `webhookEventId` dedupe inbox, async processing, `X-Line-Retry-Key` on pushes, 409 treated as success, unfollow pauses schedules) | **Keep** | All `[V]` against LINE docs on 9 Nov 2026 (omm brief §8.1, §8.8). ## 3. Conflicts and how they are resolved

| # | Conflict | omm | vj | jui | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Who the prototype is for** | Working adults in Bangkok, plus family coordinators | Diners and venue bookers in one city | University students and staff | **TEAM DECISION TD1.** Options: (A) students and staff of our university aged **20 or over, or married (sui juris)** (`LEG-05`; no under-20 testers, since that would need parental consent we do not plan to collect); (B) Bangkok working adults as omm wrote; (C) university staff and graduate students only. **Recommendation: A.** The age rule removes most first- and second-year undergraduates, so the real pool is staff, later-year and graduate students, which also makes C a sound fallback. Staff keep working adults in the sample. Report the sample honestly; it does not represent all working adults |
| C2 | **What the core is** | Obligations (bills, renewals, appointments) | Booking | 10 broad actions | **Resolved.** One "task with an outcome" engine with two task types in the prototype: an *obligation* (omm) and a *request to a business* (vj and jui). Reason: the obligation flow matches Abdul's baseline and gives the evidence-level story; the request flow is what "acts" means. Both share the lifecycle, confirmation gate, job table, and audit log, so the second type is cheaper than a separate product |
| C3 | **How we reach a business** | Drafted `oaMessage` handoff plus a follow-up | Router over five channels | Request card via share picker; reply page | **Resolved.** Default adapter (Must): `oaMessage` handoff, then "Did you send it?" and a follow-up that asks the user for the shop's answer. ## 4. What each member's work contributes, in one line

- **omm:** the backbone. The lifecycle with evidence levels, "AI interprets; code decides", the data model, LINE reliability details, the capacity plan, and the phase gates.
- **vj:** the router and venue table that make "reaching a business" an engineering component with measurable outcomes, the Wizard-of-Oz framing, the anti-goals, and the terms-of-service discipline for venue data.
- **jui:** the product model (three levels), the request card that closes the loop by tap (its reply page is Should S0 in the plan; Must when the calendar is scenario B or better), confirm-and-Edit, the university test group, and the Abdul baseline.
