# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- The team is honest about its position: it rates its own differentiation 4/10 and names the incumbent's moat as habit and context, not technology. That is the right diagnosis.
- "Verified done" versus "user-reported done" is the one idea here that competitors do not advertise. It is a seed of a secret, though not yet a product.
- Thai-specific handling (Buddhist-era dates, LINE-native bank and biller alerts) points at a local niche that global assistants serve badly.
- Accumulated personal obligations could become a switching cost, if users ever accumulate them.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It enters a crowded market as the weakest entrant.** Three products already sell LINE reminders with Google Calendar to the same buyer, one of them free with annual plans from ฿990. The confirmed scope is feature parity or less. On the engineering question this is incremental, not a tenfold improvement on anything that matters, and nothing stops a competitor copying the completion distinction in month six.
2. **No distribution.** No channel tested, no interviews, no waiting users, acquisition cost unknown. "Individual adults in Thailand" is not a small market that can be dominated; it is the whole market, where incumbents already have habit and group spread. The illustrative break-even of about 4,800 paying users has no route to the first 100.
3. **No path to any durable advantage at this price and capacity.** No network effect (family sharing is deferred), no scale economy (contribution of ฿12–62 per user, unmeasured), no brand, and full dependence on LINE and model providers. The candidate differentiators (spending import, booking) do not fit the hours available or need partnerships the team cannot get.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do you know about a specific group of Thai users that Abdul, Lamai and Cole do not? Name it in one sentence.
- Which narrow group could you win a majority of: one condominium's renters, one employer's shift workers, one university's staff? How many people are in it?
- Which obligation is painful enough that missing it costs real money, and that no existing assistant handles end to end?
- Can "externally verified done" actually be delivered for any biller, and how? If not, the claimed differentiation is a label.
- How do the first 100 users arrive, and what does each cost?
- Why now: what changed that makes this possible or needed today and not two years ago?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable; the vote is PASS as a business. As a senior project it is fine. The vote would move to FUND IF with all of the following:
- **One named small market**, countable and reachable through a single channel the team already has access to, replacing "office workers in Bangkok".
- **One obligation type done far better than any competitor**, for example verified payment of one specific bill type for that group, demonstrated side by side against Abdul on the same task.
- **Evidence of a secret**: at least 15 interviews in that group showing a problem the three competitors leave unsolved.
- **A distribution test**: 50 sign-ups from that one channel at a recorded cost, and at least 10 who pay any real price.
- **A mechanism that compounds**: a household or group feature in scope, so each user brings another, not deferred.
- **Measured variable cost per active user**, so the ฿79–99 price is known to leave a margin.
