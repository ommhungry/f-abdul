# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The core moment is concrete and lives where the user already is: forward a bill in LINE, get back a confirmed due date and amount, get reminded, mark it done. No new app, no new habit.
- The trust rules are real craft in the part nobody sees: never inventing a date or amount, keeping the source text, separating "user said done" from "verified done". Handling Thai/Buddhist-era dates is the kind of detail that decides whether the first use works.
- The team has already said no once: the committed scope is the base obligation system plus calendar, and the brief admits the spending assistant does not fit.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No one thing.** The pitch line has three clauses, and the roadmap adds spending, budgets, reimbursement, daily planning, meeting prep, booking and three personalities. As scoped today it is a reminder assistant in LINE, which the brief shows three competitors already advertise, one with a free tier and annual plans. The team's own differentiation rating is 4/10. The trust features are invisible until something goes wrong, so they do not sell the product on first use.
2. **The first five minutes is undesigned and untested.** No user interviews, no prototype, no code. Setup burden (adding the account, Google Calendar consent, confirming uncertain fields) sits in front of the first payoff, and a single wrong deadline breaks the one promise the product makes.
3. **The seams.** The experience is handed between LINE, the AI model, Google Calendar and the user's own confirmation. Each handoff can fail silently: an image the model misreads, a push message the user mutes, a calendar write that does not happen. Many Thai bank alerts never reach email, so the proposed spending feature would have a hole at its centre. A subscription at ฿79–99 leaves no room for a human to patch these seams.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence with one clause: what is the one thing, and which named competitor feature is it clearly better than?
- What exactly happens between the user forwarding their first bill and the first "this is handled" feeling, and how many taps and seconds is it?
- What share of real Thai bills (photo, PDF, LINE message, biller app screenshot) extract correctly without correction? Nobody has this number.
- What does the user see when extraction is uncertain, a reminder is missed, or the calendar write fails?
- Why would a user of a free reminder assistant switch for trust features they cannot see?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Cut to one thing:** "forward a bill, never miss it." Drop the spending assistant, booking, personalities and the other candidates from the proposal entirely, not just defer them. Rewrite the pitch as one clause.
2. **Make the trust visible.** Show the source snippet beside every extracted date and amount in the confirmation message, so the differentiation is felt in the first use, not claimed.
3. **Prove the first five minutes before building breadth.** Run 10–15 interviews with Bangkok office workers and collect their real bills; measure extraction accuracy on those and time-to-first-confirmed-obligation. Set a target in the proposal and report against it in April.
4. **Design every seam's failure state** (uncertain field, muted push, failed calendar write) as part of the committed scope, not after.
5. **Add one real demand signal** to the April evaluation: ask testers for a stated price or a waitlist sign-up, since the planned evaluation measures none.
