# Offers lens
Score: 3 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- Effort and time score well on the value equation: forward one message inside an app the buyer already uses, and get a tracked obligation back in the same chat.
- The honesty rules (no invented dates or amounts, "reported done" kept apart from "verified done") address perceived likelihood, the factor on which most offers fail.
- The team is candid: it rates its own differentiation 4/10 and marks every number as untested.
- The committed scope fits the available hours, so a real test is possible by April.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Weak market on the lens's four tests.** The pain is a nuisance, not massive: phone calendars, bank notifications and biller apps already cover it for free. Buyers are asked for ฿79–99, which signals low willingness to pay. No channel to reach them has been tested. Growth is unknown. The brief has no interview, waitlist or payment showing that anyone wants this.
2. **It is a commodity competing on price.** Abdul, Lamai and Cole advertise the same core (LINE reminders, calendar) plus more: groups, voice, phone calls, email. The one visible difference today is a lower price than Lamai and Cole, and Abdul's annual plans already undercut ฿99/month. Could the price double and still be the obvious choice? No.
3. **The price leaves no room to build a stack.** Contribution of ฿12–62 per user means any human help, guarantee or support eats the margin (two ten-minute interventions cost ฿50). Break-even at about 4,800 paying users, before acquisition cost, with no channel.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who exactly has lost money through a missed bill or renewal, how much, and how often? Name the segment (renters, freelancers, people paying for parents, small landlords).
- What does a missed obligation cost that person in baht: late fees, disconnection, lapsed insurance, a fine?
- Why would they believe it works before trying it? What proof exists by April?
- How fast is the first result: minutes from adding the LINE account to the first saved obligation?
- What guarantee could you afford to honour?
- What would you have to add for ฿199 to feel cheap?
- What is the variable cost per paying user, measured rather than assumed?
- How do the first 100 users arrive, and what does each cost?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable at PASS. The vote would move to FUND IF on evidence of the following:
- **A narrow buyer with a costly pain.** Interview at least 15 people in one segment and record what missed obligations cost them last year. Proceed only if the loss clearly exceeds a year of subscription.
- **A promise about the outcome, not the feature.** Sell "never pay a late fee again", not "reminders", and state a guarantee the margin can carry (for example, a refunded month if a confirmed deadline is missed by the product's own fault).
- **One thing rivals do not advertise.** The honesty rules only matter if buyers notice them. Test whether verified completion (proof the bill was actually paid) is something people will pay for.
- **A real price signal.** Replace part of the supervised evaluation with a pre-sale or paid waitlist at ฿99 and at ฿199, and count payments rather than opinions.
- **One tested channel.** Run a single community and report sign-ups and cost per sign-up.
- **Drop the spending assistant for now.** It does not fit the hours and widens privacy exposure before the core offer is proven.
